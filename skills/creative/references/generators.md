# Generators

Each generator is a different method for leaving the mode. Pick at least three that fit the
task; pools from different methods overlap little, which is the point. Label every idea with
the generator that made it, so a shortlist that all came from one generator is easy to see.

| Generator | The move | Good for |
|-----------|----------|----------|
| Tails sampling | Write candidates with your estimate of how likely a typical model answer each one is, then keep only those under 0.10. Asking for a distribution, not an answer, is what recovers diversity (Verbalized Sampling, arXiv 2510.01171) | Anything; strongest on names, jokes, premises |
| Distant field | Describe the task with verbs ("make strangers trust a new tool in ten seconds"), name 3-5 fields that are excellent at those verbs, and take each one's actual method, not its vocabulary. A launch, seen by a magician, a street-food vendor and a museum curator | Campaigns, product concepts, structure |
| Constraint | Add a hard, arbitrary rule and obey it: one syllable, no screens, only things that cost under $5, told backwards, no adjectives (Oulipo practice) | Writing, names, visual concepts |
| Inversion | Flip the goal or a core assumption: what would make this fail, who would hate it, what if the product did the opposite of its job | Campaigns, positioning, plots |
| Raw material | Mine the specifics: the product's odd features, the user's data, the place, a real customer complaint, the commit history, etc. Real details are hard to make generic | Copy, names, stories, pitches |
| Random entry | Pull an unrelated word or object (from the room, a dictionary page, a news headline) and force a connection (de Bono's random entry) | Getting unstuck, names |
| Combination | Collide two earlier ideas from different generators; keep the result only if it is stronger than either | Second pass on any pool |
| Audience swap | Design it for a different, specific person (a child, a skeptic, a competitor's loyal customer), then bring back what transfers | Messaging, UX concepts |
| Exaggerate | Push one property to an absurd size, then step back to the largest version that still works | Hooks, product features |

## Writing tells

For prose, grep the draft and replace each hit with the specific thing it stands in for:

`delve, tapestry, testament, realm, vibrant, bustling, whisper(ed|s), symphony of, dance of,
a sense of, palpable, intricate, nestled, beacon, embark, journey (as metaphor), in a world
where, little did, it's not just X it's Y`

The tell is density, not a single word: several of these in one paragraph means the
writing reached for the typical phrase instead of the observed detail. Other structural
tells: three-item lists everywhere, every paragraph ending on a moral, and an em dash or
colon reveal in each sentence.
